## 1. What to produce

A complete brand identity for **Rodgate Group**, deliverable as:

1. **Logo** — primary, horizontal, stacked, and a single-letter/monogram mark. Must work at 16px (favicon,
   email avatar) and on a 3ft truck decal. **Must survive 1-color black, 1-color white, and fax-quality B&W** —
   federal paperwork still gets photocopied and faxed.
2. **Color system** — primary, secondary, one accent, plus neutrals. WCAG AA minimum on all text pairings.
   Include exact HEX + RGB + CMYK (print matters — proposals get printed and mailed).
3. **Typography** — a heading face and a body face. **Both must have a free/open license** and be available
   in Google Fonts or system-safe, because documents get opened by government staff on locked-down machines.
   Include a Word/Google-Docs fallback stack.
4. **Document system** — proposal cover page, capability statement (1 page), letterhead, email signature.
   These are the highest-value assets: they're what a contracting officer actually sees.
5. **Applications** — business card, polo/uniform embroidery mark, vehicle decal, job-site sign.
6. **Usage rules** — clear space, minimum sizes, what not to do.

## 3. Who it sells to — read this before choosing anything

**The buyer is a federal or state contracting officer.** Not a consumer, not a startup founder.

This single fact should govern the whole system:

- They evaluate on **responsibility and risk**, not personality. The brand's job is to make a small company
  look like a *safe award* — organized, established, compliant.
- Their world is **printed and photocopied**. Forms, faxes, black-and-white. A gradient-heavy identity that
  only lives in RGB will fail in the one place it's judged.
- **Trendy reads as risky here.** Startup-flashy (neon, glassmorphism, playful lowercase, illustrated
  mascots) actively hurts credibility with this buyer. Nothing that looks like a 2026 SaaS.
- The secondary audience is **prime contractors** looking for small-business subs to meet their subcontracting
  goals, and **subcontractors** deciding whether to quote. Both are evaluating whether this outfit is real.

**The emotional target:** *"These people will show up, do the work, and not become my problem."*
Competence and steadiness. Not excitement.

## 4. Voice (documented, not invented)

Built from a real corpus of the founder's writing. Two registers:

**Public voice** — plain, direct, warm, honest. Short sentences with line breaks between thoughts. Talks to
one person ("you"). Names the hard part before the lesson. Sparing, purposeful emoji.

**Professional/GovCon voice** — the same directness, formality up, **no emoji at all**, no hype that can't be
backed. Claims are only made when they're literally true (this company runs a hard rule against overstating
certifications or past performance).

His own sales doctrine, which the copy in any brand deliverable should respect:
1. Diagnose before you pitch — open on the reader's problem, not the product.
2. Sell the **outcome**, not the thing.
3. Price once, then stop justifying.
4. Objections are signals — answer the top 2–3 before they're raised.

**Tone words:** direct · steady · accountable · plainspoken · earned.
**Not:** clever, edgy, corporate-vague, salesy, "innovative", "world-class".

## 5. Constraints and cautions

- **Do not invent credentials.** Not 8(a), not SDVOSB, not WOSB, not HUBZone. The real list is in §2 and
  claiming anything beyond it is a false certification with legal consequence.
- **The name is "Rodgate"** — one word, capital R. "Rodgate Group" in full. Never "RodGate" in the logo.
- **Avoid the cleaning-industry clichés**: sparkles, bubbles, a swoosh mop, a shining star, aggressive
  greens. Every janitorial company uses these, and they read as *residential*. This company bids federal
  facilities contracts.
- **"Gate" is in the name** — a gate, an arch, a threshold, a keystone is a legitimate and non-obvious visual
  route. Pennsylvania is the Keystone State, which makes a keystone form doubly apt. Explore it, don't force it.
- Color note: heavy blue is the default for every govcon shop and disappears in a stack of proposals.
  Something quieter and more distinctive that still reads institutional would serve better — but AA contrast
  and B&W survivability come first.
